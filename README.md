# Time Series Forecasting for AI Systems

**السلاسل الزمنية والتنبؤ** · SDAIA Academy · three days

A three-day capstone course on forecasting: decomposing a series and reading
its autocorrelation, fitting classical models (ARIMA/SARIMA, exponential
smoothing), engineering features for a gradient-boosted learner, validating
with walk-forward backtesting, and producing calibrated prediction intervals.
Four tools carry the course end to end — **statsmodels**, **Prophet**,
**sktime**, and **LightGBM** — each earning its place against a use case it
actually fits: demand planning, workforce projections, and an economic
indicator.

**[Visit the site](https://mohammadyusif.github.io/time-series-forecasting-ai-systems/)**

## Why these four datasets

Every lesson and lab runs against one of four synthetic datasets in `data/`,
generated by `data/generate_series.py` and each chosen to force a different
failure mode the moment a new technique is introduced:

- **`retail_demand.csv`** — three years of daily sales across three regions
  and two categories. Trend, weekly and yearly seasonality, and sporadic
  promo shocks make it the main running example through Day 1 and Day 2.
- **`workforce_demand.csv`** — daily required headcount with a sustained
  ~35% structural break partway through. A model that backtests well right
  up to the break and then fails is Day 3's case for watching a series after
  deployment, not just at validation time.
- **`economic_indicator.csv`** — monthly, 108 points back to 2017, a slow
  business cycle riding a trend, and a 2020-shaped shock with a partial
  recovery. Short and low-frequency by design — the case for what a model
  can still say with a decade of monthly points instead of years of daily
  ones.
- **`intermittent_demand.csv`** — four SKUs, daily, ~95% zero-valued rows.
  MAPE breaks on it; WAPE doesn't. It's the dataset behind "pick the metric
  that fits the data's scale" and half the model-comparison discussion on
  Day 3.

None of it is real. Every number produced from these files describes a
synthetic series, not any actual retailer, employer, or government
indicator.

## Runs with no API key

Nothing in this course needs an API key, a GPU, or a paid service. The
datasets are generated from a fixed seed by `data/generate_series.py` —
re-running it reproduces the four CSVs byte-for-byte — and every lab
notebook installs its own (free, open-source) packages and downloads the
data and shared utility files straight from this repository, so a fresh
Colab runtime with nothing cloned still works.

## Building the site locally

This is a [Quarto](https://quarto.org) website. The lab notebooks are
committed with their executed outputs, so rendering does not re-execute them
or require the forecasting libraries to be installed:

```
quarto render
quarto preview
```
